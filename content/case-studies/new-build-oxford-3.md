## The brief

Sutton Builders acquired a prominent corner plot on the edge of an established Oxford suburb and decided to use it as a show home for a wider development of four-bedroom detached houses. The site sat within the OX1 and OX2 delivery zones, close to busy arterial routes and within sight of two residential streets. Because the property was intended as a marketing suite, the finish had to be immaculate and the programme had to hit a spring launch date that had already been advertised to estate agents and prospective buyers.

The house was designed as a four-bedroom detached dwelling with a part brick, part rendered façade, a large feature gable window and an integral double garage. Sutton Builders wanted a single supplier to price the structural envelope from the foundation sub-base to the roof insulation, then deliver it in phased drops that followed the site programme. They were clear that the show home was the sales tool for the rest of the site: any delay would push back the launch event and undermine pre-launch marketing.

After receiving the architect's bill of quantities, we produced a fully itemised quote within twenty-four hours and assigned a named scheduler who would stay with the project for the full nine months. The quote was broken down by build phase so Sutton Builders could see exactly when each material would be needed and what it would cost. The quote covered every structural item we could supply, flagged the items outside our range, and set out a proposed delivery calendar tied to the trades. Sutton Builders used this calendar as the basis of their own build programme, which reduced the risk of mismatched deliveries.

## The challenge

Three pressures shaped the supply plan from the first site meeting.

1. **Immovable launch deadline.** The show home had to be finished, landscaped and dressed for a launch event in April. Invitations had been sent, the sales team had been hired and the marketing photography was booked. Any slip in the structural programme would compress the finishing trades and risk missing the launch window.

2. **Corner-plot visibility and access.** The plot sat on the junction of two residential roads with no space for a long-term material compound. Both frontages were visible to passers-by, so pallets and wrapping had to be cleared quickly. Deliveries also had to avoid the morning and afternoon school runs, which made the roads busy and narrowed the available parking.

3. **Mixed brick-and-render façade.** The front and side elevations were to be built in Hampton Rural Blend Brick, while the rear and gables were to receive a render finish. We needed to coordinate the brick deliveries with the bricklayer's output and supply the base materials for the render backing coat without taking responsibility for the specialist render finish itself.

Sutton Builders also wanted the site to look tidy at all times because potential buyers would be walking past before the launch. They asked us to avoid overnight stockpiles and to keep any unavoidable materials behind hoardings. That meant clean pallets, same-day wrapping removal and a delivery rhythm that never left unsightly stockpiles on the pavement.

## Materials we supplied

We priced the full structural envelope as one integrated package and then split it into eight staged drops. The materials we supplied were:

- **Sub-base and concrete:** 29 tonnes of **MOT Type 1 Aggregate**, 14 tonnes of **20mm Ballast Aggregate**, 24 tonnes of **Building Sand** and 181 bags of **General Purpose Cement** for the foundations, ground slab, floor screeds and mortar.
- **Masonry walls:** 1,063 **Aircrete Blocks** for the inner leaf and partition walls, and 5,506 **Hampton Rural Blend Bricks** for the outer leaf and feature gables.
- **Openings:** 15 **Standard 100mm Cavity Lintels** for windows, doors and the garage opening.
- **Timber and sheet:** 1.9 cubic metres of **C24 Timber**, 119 linear metres of **BS Treated Battens**, 26 sheets of 18mm **OSB3 Board** and 10 sheets of **Premium Hardwood Plywood**.
- **Insulation:** 56 sheets of **Cavity Insulation Slab** and 65 sheets of **PIR Insulation Board** for the cavity walls, roof and threshold details.
- **Fixings:** 11 boxes of **Galvanized Nails** and 4 boxes of **High Performance Wood Screws**.

The brick order was split into three drops so the site never held more than the bricklayer could lay in a fortnight. The blocks arrived in two drops to keep the storage area clear, and the timber was cut to length off site so it could be lifted directly onto the scaffold. Insulation was scheduled to arrive just before the walls and roof were closed in, protecting it from site damage and weather. Any damaged sheets were replaced free of charge on the next drop.

## Delivery and logistics

Show-home builds reward disciplined logistics. We structured the Oxford deliveries around four rules.

- **Just-in-time drops.** Every delivery on the Oxford new-build was tied to the following week's work. If the bricklayer was delayed by a day, the brick drop moved with it. The site manager updated the delivery calendar every Friday after the weekly programme meeting. Our scheduler confirmed the next two weeks' drops by email so both sides had a written record.
- **School-run avoidance.** No deliveries were booked between 8am and 9.15am or between 2.45pm and 3.45pm. Bulk aggregates arrived at 7am, and palletised goods arrived at 9.30am. This staggered approach kept the entrance clear and prevented two lorries from blocking each other.
- **Clean site protocol.** Pallets, banding and wrapping were removed on the return leg. Because the plot was visible from two roads, keeping the perimeter tidy was treated as part of the marketing strategy.
- **Single point of contact.** Sutton's site manager had one named scheduler at our trade counter for the whole programme. The scheduler attended the weekly programme meeting by phone and could rebook drops within 24 hours when trades shifted.

We also pre-staged a small reserve of cement, nails and screws at our yard so that minor variations did not require a full merchant run. This reserve meant the site manager could collect or request a small drop the same day instead of waiting for a full load. The reserve was called on twice: once when the garage opening was widened and once when additional noggins were needed for the gable window framing.

## The build

The build ran for nine months, which we broke into a weekly programme to keep the trades aligned.

- **Weeks 1–3: Site set-up and groundworks.** The existing outbuildings were demolished, the site was levelled and the utilities were diverted. Trenches were dug for the foundations and the ground-bearing slab was prepared. The first drop of MOT Type 1, ballast, sand and cement arrived at 7am on the Monday of week two.
- **Weeks 4–6: Foundations and ground slab.** Reinforcement was placed, the foundations were poured and the ground-floor slab was cast. The DPM and insulation under the slab were installed before the slab concrete went in.
- **Weeks 7–12: Sub-structure walls.** The inner leaf of aircrete blocks rose first, followed by the outer leaf of Hampton Rural Blend Brick. Cavity insulation slabs were fitted as the walls went up, and cavity trays were bedded over each lintel. The 15 cavity lintels were delivered in two batches to match the opening programme.
- **Weeks 13–16: Roof structure and windows.** The C24 timber roof structure was assembled, the OSB3 deck was fixed and the battens were laid. The large gable window was installed once the opening was framed and weathered.
- **Weeks 17–22: First fix and plastering.** The electricians and plumbers ran their services, the plasterboard was fixed and the walls were skimmed. The PIR insulation board was fitted at the roof junctions and threshold details.
- **Weeks 23–28: Second fix and external finishes.** Kitchens, bathrooms and internal doors went in. The render backing coat was applied to the rear and gables, and the external brickwork was cleaned down.
- **Weeks 29–34: Decoration and joinery.** Final decoration, flooring, tiling and external joinery were completed. The double garage doors were hung and the driveway sub-base was laid.
- **Weeks 35–39: Landscaping, dressing and launch.** The garden was planted, the show home was dressed by the interior stylist and the launch event took place on the planned Saturday morning. The build finished on the agreed date, and the site was handed over cleanly, on time and on budget.

The only significant adjustment came in week ten, when heavy rain delayed the brickwork by two days. Because the insulation and timber were not delivered until they were needed, nothing was left standing in the wet, and the programme recovered by week twelve. The scheduler moved the next insulation drop by three days to match the revised brickwork date, so no space was wasted on site. The bricklayer caught up over the following fine week and the launch date remained unchanged.
