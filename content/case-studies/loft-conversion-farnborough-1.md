## The challenge

Every loft conversion with rear dormer has three pressure points: getting the materials in, keeping them in the right order, and making sure someone answers the phone when the site needs to change a drop. On this Farnborough job we addressed all three before the groundworks started.

1. **Access.** Most of the structural timber had to be carried through the house and up through the staircase opening, so we split the C24 drop into smaller bundles that two people could move safely. There was no driveway for a 7.5-tonne lorry, so we used a transit-tipper and pre-phoned the homeowner to move their car. We also avoided delivery windows during the school run, when the surrounding Farnborough roads are at a standstill.

2. **Sequencing.** A loft conversion with rear dormer is a multi-stage job. We split the schedule into 4 matched drops so the site never had surplus material blocking access, and each trade had exactly what they needed for the weeks ahead.

3. **Same-day contact.** Every loft order in Farnborough went through one named scheduler at Star Hawk, not a national call centre. Changes made before 11am were acknowledged within the hour and, for bulk aggregates and bagged cement, often on site the same afternoon.

Once the scaffold went up, every timber, board and insulation drop had a slot. The roofer and carpenter could work continuously, which is critical when the roof is open to the weather.

### Why these three points matter

Loft conversions live or die by roof-level coordination. If the timber arrives before the steels are in, the site becomes unworkable; if it arrives late, the roof stays open. The three-point plan made sure the right materials reached the loft at the right moment.

### How to avoid the same issues on your project

Start the material conversation as soon as the drawings are ready, not after the digger arrives. Share the programme with your supplier and build in a standing reserve for the items that always run short. Finally, nominate one person on site who speaks to one person at the supplier — that single thread saves more time than any app.

## The build

The build ran for 7 weeks. Below is the diary of how the programme unfolded.

### Pre-start

Before the scaffold went up, the loft schedule was loaded into our system with drops tied to strip-out, steels, framing, roofing and internal finish. The carpenter and scheduler shared the same plan.

### Weekly diary

- **Week 1: Scaffold, strip roof and steels.** The scaffold went up and the roof tiles were stripped back. Structural steels were threaded into the loft to support the new dormer cheeks. C24 timber was delivered in smaller bundles so it could be carried through the house. By the end of week 1, the programme was on track for the next stage and any shortages were recorded on the delivery note.

- **Week 2: Rafters and dormer framing.** C24 rafters were cut and fixed, and the dormer cheeks were framed in timber. The carpenter worked from a cutting list so every length matched the architect's roof plan. The roof opening was covered with a temporary tarp at the end of each day. By the end of week 2, the programme was on track for the next stage and any shortages were recorded on the delivery note.

- **Week 3: Sheathing, felt and battens.** OSB sheathing was fixed to the dormer cheeks and roof deck, followed by breathable membrane and treated battens. This weatherproofed the opening quickly, which is essential in Farnborough. The same drop included extra battens for the roofer's cuts. By the end of week 3, the programme was on track for the next stage and any shortages were recorded on the delivery note.

- **Week 4: Tiling and windows.** Roof tiles were laid and the dry ridge kit fitted along the new dormer ridge. Velux or dormer windows were installed and flashed. The loft was now watertight and the internal trades could start. By the end of week 4, the programme was on track for the next stage and any shortages were recorded on the delivery note.

- **Week 5: Insulation and plasterboard.** PIR insulation boards were cut and fitted between rafters and on the dormer cheeks, with **loft roll** packed into the remaining voids. Plasterboard was screwed to the new walls and ceiling. Air-tightness tape was applied at junctions. By the end of week 5, the programme was on track for the next stage and any shortages were recorded on the delivery note.

- **Week 6: First fix and plaster.** First-fix electrics and plumbing were run through the floor and walls. The plasterers skimmed the boards, giving the room a clean finish. The staircase opening was protected while the plaster cured. By the end of week 6, the programme was on track for the next stage and any shortages were recorded on the delivery note.

- **Week 7: Second fix and handover.** Second-fix carpentry, decoration and final cleaning were completed. The new staircase was handed over by the joinery specialist. The homeowners moved into the finished master suite. By the end of week 7, the programme was on track for the next stage and any shortages were recorded on the delivery note.

### Programme controls

If the steels were fitted early, the timber drop moved forward; if the roofers lost a day to rain, the tile delivery was delayed. That flexibility kept the loft watertight and the site uncluttered.

### Weather and quality checks

We watched the weather closely because an open roof is vulnerable. Sheet materials were kept dry under covers, and the carpenter signed every delivery note before the timber went into the loft.

### Snagging and handover

By the end of 7 weeks the loft was ready for the finishing trades. The staircase opening was made safe, surplus timber was collected, and Wheatley Homes signed off the final delivery note.

## What we don't supply on a loft-conversion

To save you a wasted call, here is what we do not supply on a loft-conversion project like this one in Farnborough:

### Items outside our range

- The finished staircase on the Farnborough loft — we supply structural timbers, but the staircase itself is a specialist joinery item.
- Velux, dormer or roof windows — we can recommend a local fitter who will trade-price the supply and installation.
- Electrical first- and second-fix work, plumbing and heating — these come from separate trade suppliers.
- Bathroom sanitaryware, tiles and wet-room tanking — usually supplied by the bathroom fitter.
- Carpets, floor finishes, decoration and blinds — the final fit-out is a separate supply chain.
- Scaffolding and access equipment — this is hired directly by the builder or roofing contractor.
- Waste disposal, skip hire and site-clearance labour — arranged by the main contractor.
- Final cleaning, snagging decoration and window cleaning — usually handled by the finishing trade.

### What this means for your schedule
