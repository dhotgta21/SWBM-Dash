## The brief

Little Chalfont Homes was asked to build a garden outbuilding with a WC in a Bracknell garden. The clients wanted a permanent, insulated structure that could serve as a pool house, changing room and utility space, with a toilet and small basin for convenience. The plot was enclosed on three sides by fencing and tall planting, and the only access was a narrow side path between the house and the boundary wall.

We visited the site in early summer, measured the path width and the clearance around the proposed building, and priced the base, insulation, lining and fixings as one pack. Because the outbuilding included a WC, the concrete base had to be thick enough to support drainage channels and the walls had to be robust enough for concealed soil pipework.

The quote was issued within three days and covered all the structural materials listed in the specification. The clients signed off the quote before the ground was broken, and the programme was set at six weeks to complete before the children returned to school in September.

The clients wanted the outbuilding to last as long as the main house, so the base, insulation and vapour control were specified to residential standards. They were looking for a robust utility space, not a lightweight summer building that would need replacing within a decade. They also wanted the outbuilding to match the main house visually, so the external finish, door style and roof covering were chosen to complement the existing property, even though those items were supplied by other merchants.

## The challenge

The Bracknell outbuilding had four practical challenges. The first was access. The side path was only just wide enough for a wheelbarrow and turned sharply at a downpipe, so all materials had to be carried through by hand. We therefore planned small, frequent drops rather than a single bulk delivery.

The second challenge was the WC. The outbuilding needed a soil connection to the main house drainage, which meant the concrete base had to include the correct falls and a channel for the pipe. The base was also larger and thicker than a typical garden room, which explains the 27 tonnes of MOT Type 1 and the 158 bags of cement needed for the slab and footings.

The third challenge was the summer weather. The build was scheduled for August, when Bracknell can see heavy showers followed by strong sun. Materials had to be protected from rain and from warping in the heat, particularly the plasterboard and PIR boards.

The fourth challenge was the neighbours. The path ran alongside the boundary fence, and the neighbouring property had young children who used the garden during the day. We therefore booked deliveries for mid-morning, kept the path clear of obstacles, and made sure noisy work was completed early in the day.

The soil in the Bracknell garden was a heavy clay that held water after rain, so the base had to be raised above the lawn level and include a French drain to keep the sub-base dry. We allowed for this in the quote and made sure the MOT Type 1 was delivered in a single drop so the groundworker could complete the drainage layer without interruption. The raised base also created a small step up to the outbuilding, which the clients later finished with decking supplied by another contractor.

## Materials we supplied

Little Chalfont Homes sent us the specification and we priced the structural pack in one quote, then split it into five small drops to suit the Bracknell access. The quantities we supplied were:

- **Foundations and sub-base:** 27 tonnes of **MOT Type 1 Aggregate**, 158 bags of **General Purpose Cement**, 25 tonnes of **Building Sand**, 10 tonnes of **20mm Ballast Aggregate**.
- **Insulation and linings:** 8 sheets of **Standard Plasterboards**, 11 sheets of **PIR Insulation Board**.
- **Fixings and hardware:** 11 boxes of **Galvanized Nails**, 2 boxes of **High Performance Wood Screws**.

The high quantity of aggregates and cement reflects the substantial base required for the WC drainage and the overall size of the outbuilding. The PIR boards were used in the walls and roof to keep the space warm, and the plasterboard provided the internal finish. The nails were used for the timber frame and battens, while the screws fixed the linings and any joinery. Because the outbuilding included a WC, the floor slab was thicker than a standard garden room and included a fall towards the soil outlet, which increased the concrete volume and required more ballast and cement than the footprint alone would suggest. We therefore allowed for a higher cement-to-aggregate ratio in the quote to make sure the concrete would be strong enough to support the drainage channels without cracking.

Because the path was narrow, all sheet materials were cut where possible before delivery to sizes that two people could carry through the gate. The cement was shrink-wrapped on pallets to prevent spills, and the aggregates were tipped on the driveway and barrowed through in small loads.

## Delivery and logistics

We structured the Bracknell deliveries around the six-week programme and the restricted access. Drop one brought the MOT Type 1, cement, sand and ballast for the base, tipped on the driveway and moved through by wheelbarrow. Drop two delivered the timber frame and battens, carried through in manageable lengths. Drop three brought the PIR insulation and plasterboard on a dry day. Drop four delivered the fixings and a small top-up of cement. Drop five was a final collection of empty pallets and surplus materials.

The path from the gate to the base was protected with old scaffold boards laid on heavy-duty polythene. The boards were lifted each evening so the family could still use the path, and the polythene was swept clean before the weekend. Because the path turned sharply at a rainwater downpipe, the boards were cut to fit around the bend rather than simply laid straight, which prevented the lawn from being worn at the corner.

Because the drops were small, we combined several items onto a single 3.5-tonne tipper and then carried them through by hand. The driver helped lift the heavier items over the gate and stacked them neatly on the base slab so the carpenters could start work immediately.

The site manager called our trade counter the evening before each Bracknell drop to confirm the timing and to warn us if rain had made the garden too soft. On one occasion a forecast of thunderstorms led us to delay a plasterboard drop by 24 hours, holding the boards at our yard rather than risking them in a downpour.

A small reserve of cement and nails was kept at our yard against Little Chalfont Homes' account. When the groundworker needed an extra bag of cement to level one corner of the base after the formwork shifted slightly, it arrived the same afternoon on a vehicle already in Bracknell. The reserve was also used when the carpenter needed an extra box of nails after the timber frame proved more complex than the initial cut list suggested.

## The build

The garden outbuilding in Bracknell ran for six weeks from the first excavation to handover. Week one was the excavation, MOT Type 1 sub-base and the reinforced concrete slab, with drainage channels formed during the pour. Week two saw the timber frame erected and the roof structure installed. Week three was the PIR insulation, breathable membrane and external cladding supplied by another merchant. Week four was the plasterboard lining and the first-fix plumbing. Weeks five and six were the WC installation, decoration and external landscaping.

Our materials were used at every structural stage. The MOT Type 1, cement, sand and ballast formed the base. The PIR boards insulated the walls and roof. The plasterboard lined the interior. The nails and screws fixed the frame, linings and joinery.

The site manager kept a simple programme on a whiteboard in the kitchen and updated it each afternoon. When the groundworker finished the slab two days early, we moved the frame delivery forward so the carpenters could keep their momentum. When the plumber needed an extra day to route the soil pipe, we held the plasterboard drop back.

Building control did not need to visit because the outbuilding fell within permitted development, but the site manager kept our delivery notes and insulation certificates on file in case the clients needed them for a future sale or insurance valuation.

## The result

The completed outbuilding is a permanent, insulated structure at the end of the Bracknell garden. It contains a changing area, storage space and a fully functional WC, all on a concrete base that will not move or dampen over time. The clients use it daily during the summer and as a utility space in the winter. The insulation has kept the interior above freezing even on cold nights, and the plasterboard finish gives it the appearance of a room rather than a shed.

Little Chalfont Homes handed over on the agreed six-week date. The family commented that the build had caused less disruption than expected, despite the narrow access, and the garden recovered quickly once the protective boarding was removed. The WC has been particularly useful during summer barbecues and pool parties, and the clients have since asked the builder to price a second, smaller storage shed using the same delivery method.

The Star Hawk structural and insulation pack for this Bracknell outbuilding was included in the overall contract at £4,260, with Little Chalfont Homes recovering the cost through the project. The value was in the careful sequencing that kept a compact site moving without blocking the path or disturbing the neighbours.
