## The brief

Stoneridge Construction was asked to build a garden office at the end of a long garden in Hungerford for a client who had started working from home permanently after changing jobs. The clients were a couple running a small design consultancy from a spare bedroom, and the arrangement had become unsustainable during the winter months when one needed quiet space for video calls and the other needed room for drawing boards and sample storage. The brief was for a warm, quiet, self-contained room with space for a desk, a meeting area and a small kitchenette, with a design that looked like a deliberate addition to the garden rather than a shed.

The clients wanted a timber-frame building with cedar cladding, a sedum or pitched roof and a large corner window overlooking the lawn. The office needed to be usable all year round, which meant high levels of insulation, a small heater, proper cable routes for power and data, and a design that would not overheat in summer sun. The garden was level but narrow, with mature trees along one boundary and a vegetable patch that had to stay in place. Access was through a side gate and down a gravel path, which meant no large vehicles and no materials left on the lawn for long. Stoneridge also wanted to avoid damaging the yew hedge, which was well established and formed part of the garden's character.

The design evolved over several conversations. The clients initially considered a simple pod but soon realised they needed proper wall and roof insulation, a services void and a floor that could take office furniture and occasional client visits. They chose cedar cladding because it would weather to a silver-grey that matched the garden fences, and they wanted the sedum roof to be visible from the upstairs windows of the main house. Stoneridge needed the base, frame, insulation, boards and fixings delivered in a sequence that kept the garden usable and protected the trees.

They called us before the design was finalised and we priced the full structural pack from a sketch, identifying the cedar cladding, sedum roof, windows, electrics and kitchenette as specialist packages. The quote was broken down by build stage so the clients could see the cost of the base, frame, insulation and linings separately. This transparency helped them decide where to invest and where to keep things simple.

## The challenge

Garden offices in rural gardens always bring a particular set of pressures. On this Hungerford job we had to manage access, tree protection, weather and the clients' desire for a high-quality finish, all within a six-week programme.

**Access.** The side gate was just over a metre wide and opened onto a gravel path between a yew hedge and the house. A mini-digger could not get through, so the foundation excavation was done by hand and every bag, block and sheet had to be carried in. We used a 3.5-tonne tipper at the front and the team shuttled materials to the back in a wheelbarrow and a powered barrow. Plywood sheets were laid along the path to protect the gravel from ruts.

**Tree protection.** The garden had mature oak and ash trees close to the building line. The clients were concerned about root damage, so the foundation was designed as a floating concrete slab with a reduced excavation depth. We supplied the sub-base and concrete in small quantities so the ground was not disturbed more than necessary. No heavy plant was used near the root protection zones.

**Weather.** The build started in April, when Wiltshire can still have frosts and heavy showers. We scheduled the concrete pour for a dry day and made sure the timber and sheet materials were covered if they could not be installed immediately. The sheet materials were never left uncovered overnight.

**Flat-roof detailing.** The client wanted a sedum roof, which meant the roof structure had to be strong enough to carry the extra load of soil and plants and the waterproofing had to be perfect. We supplied the C24 timber, OSB and plywood for the roof deck and worked with the roofing contractor to make sure the falls were built into the structure so water would drain to the gutter.

**Power and data.** The office needed mains power, data cabling and a small kitchenette. We made sure the insulation and plasterboard were installed with cable routes in mind, even though the electrics were supplied by a separate contractor. Service voids were left for the kitchenette plumbing and waste.

**Finish expectations.** Because the office was visible from the main house and the patio, the clients wanted clean lines and no visible fixings. We supplied hardwood plywood for the internal reveals and made sure the OSB was fully covered so no rough edges showed through. The timber was selected for straight grain and minimal knots.

**Same-day contact.** Stoneridge's site manager had one named scheduler at Star Hawk. When the forecast changed and the concrete pour had to be brought forward by a day, we rearranged the cement and ballast drop with 24 hours' notice.

## Delivery and logistics

Garden offices in sensitive gardens need a light touch. We structured the Hungerford drops around four priorities: keep the path clear, protect the trees, time the weather-sensitive materials, and hold reserve stock for the inevitable adjustment.

**First drop — base materials.** On the Hungerford project, MOT Type 1, ballast, sand and cement arrived in small quantities over two days. The team carried them through the side gate and stacked them on boards near the building footprint. Plywood sheets were laid along the gravel path to protect it from the wheelbarrow traffic. The cement was covered with a tarp each evening in case of rain.

**Second drop — timber and sheet materials.** The C24 timber, battens, OSB and plywood arrived once the slab had cured. The timber was cut to length in our yard so the frame could be assembled quickly. The sheet materials were covered with a breathable tarp at the front and carried through two at a time. The OSB was stored on bearers to keep it off the damp ground.

**Third drop — insulation and plasterboard.** The PIR insulation and plasterboard arrived once the frame was weathered. Because these materials are vulnerable to moisture, we timed the drop for a dry morning and stored the boards inside the building as they arrived.

**Reserve stock.** We held extra boxes of screws and a spare pack of battens at the yard. When the team discovered that the corner window needed extra framing, the additional timber was on site the next morning. This reserve meant the frame could be completed without waiting for a second timber delivery.

All deliveries were booked for 9.30am after the school run. The driver helped stack materials at the front gate and the site manager confirmed each drop the evening before. If rain was forecast, the driver carried extra sheeting to protect the load during unloading.

## The build

The build ran for 6 weeks, with a clear weekly programme that kept the garden office on track.

**Week 1: Excavation and slab.** The ground was excavated by hand to a reduced depth to protect the tree roots. A DPM was laid, reinforcement was placed and the floating concrete slab was poured in a single day. The slab was left to cure for three days before the frame was erected.

**Week 2: Frame and floor deck.** The timber frame was erected on the slab and the OSB floor deck was fixed. The wall panels were squared and braced before the roof joists were levelled to the designed falls for the sedum roof.

**Week 3: Roof and windows.** The roof deck was covered with membrane and battens, the corner window and door were installed and the building became weather-tight. The cedar cladding started going on and the breathable membrane was checked for tears before the boards were fixed.

**Week 4: Insulation and boarding.** PIR insulation was fitted tightly between studs and rafters, a vapour control layer was installed and plasterboard was fixed to the walls and ceiling. Tape and jointing was completed ready for painting.

**Week 5: Electrics and second fix.** Wiring, sockets, lighting, data points and a small wall heater were installed. The kitchenette units were fitted and the data cables were tested. The plasterboard was painted with a low-VOC paint to minimise odours before handover.

**Week 6: Finishing and handover.** The sedum roof was laid, the floor was finished, the interior was painted and the clients moved in their desks. The garden was tidied, the path was re-gravelled where necessary and Stoneridge demonstrated the heating controls.

The build finished on time. The only delay was one day when heavy rain made the concrete pour unsafe, but the team used the time to prepare the frame components so no overall time was lost.

## The result

The finished garden office is a 16 sq m timber-framed building with cedar cladding, a green sedum roof, a large corner window and a compact kitchenette. It is warm enough for year-round use, quiet enough for video calls and looks like a natural part of the garden. The sedum roof is visible from the upstairs windows of the main house and blends into the surrounding greenery.

Stoneridge billed the clients £32,000 for the build, which included the cedar cladding, sedum roof, glazing, electrics, kitchenette, floor finish and labour. The Star Hawk material pack was £6,400.

For a material spend of £6,400, the value was in the coordination. By cutting the timber to length, timing the sheet deliveries around the weather and keeping the hand-carry site moving, we helped Stoneridge finish the office in six weeks without damaging the garden. The clients were able to start working from the office the Monday after handover and have since held several client meetings there.
