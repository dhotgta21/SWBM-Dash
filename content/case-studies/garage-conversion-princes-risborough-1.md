## The brief

Widmer End Construction was asked to convert an integral single garage in Princes Risborough into a home gym. The owners were a couple who worked from home and wanted a dedicated workout space with a rowing machine, weights area and a wall-mounted TV, without losing a room in the main house. The garage was a single-skin brick structure with a cold concrete floor, a metal up-and-over door and no insulation. In winter the room was barely above outside temperature, and in summer it was hot and stuffy. The couple had used it for storage but wanted to reclaim the space for something more useful.

The brief was to insulate the walls and roof, board the interior, install rubber flooring and add power and heating so the space could be used in winter. The owners also wanted a mirrored wall and a small fan for ventilation. Because the garage was integral to the house, the work had to be carried out with minimal disruption. The couple had considered building a garden gym room but decided that converting the existing garage would be cheaper, quicker and less likely to require planning permission. The owners were on video calls during the day, so noisy work and deliveries had to be timed carefully.

Widmer End called us with the drawings and we priced the conversion pack. The Star Hawk line was modest — just fixings — but the reliability of the supply was critical because the family was at home throughout and any delay would have pushed the handover date back. The couple had set a target of six weeks because they wanted the gym ready before the autumn, when motivation for outdoor exercise tends to drop. They also wanted the work completed before they hosted a family weekend in September.

## The challenge

Garage conversions to home gyms always create a particular set of pressures. On this Princes Risborough job we had to manage access, floor loading, ventilation and the occupied house.

**Integral garage.** Because the garage shared a wall with the house, dust and noise had to be controlled. The connecting door into the hallway needed a proper seal to keep gym smells and noise out of the living area. We supplied the fixings for the new door lining and advised on the acoustic seal, although the seal itself came from the joinery supplier.

**Floor loading.** The owners wanted a rowing machine and a weights rack. The existing concrete slab was sound but uneven, so it had to be levelled before the rubber gym flooring could be laid. We supplied extra screws for fixing the levelling compound battens. The weights rack added point loads, so the frame around the equipment area was reinforced with extra timber.

**Ventilation.** A gym in a small garage generates moisture. We made sure the insulation was fitted with a vapour control layer and advised on the positioning of a through-wall vent, even though the vent itself was supplied by the electrician. The vent was positioned high on the rear wall so it would draw air across the room without creating a draught directly over the equipment.

**Occupied house.** The owners worked from home with frequent video calls. Deliveries were kept to weekday mornings and noisy work such as fixing plasterboard was scheduled around call times. The foreman texted the owners each evening with the next day's noisy-work schedule so they could plan their calls accordingly. This simple routine kept the relationship smooth and meant the owners never had to reschedule an important call because of unexpected drilling.

**Mirrored wall.** The owners wanted a full-height mirror on one wall, which required a flat, well-fixed surface. We supplied extra screws and battens to make sure the plasterboard could carry the mirror weight. The mirror was 2.4 metres wide and had to be fixed at multiple points to prevent flexing when the room was in use.

**Same-day contact.** Widmer End's foreman had one named scheduler at Star Hawk. When the plasterer on the Princes Risborough garage conversion needed an extra pack of screws on a Friday afternoon, we had them delivered within two hours. The foreman also had a direct mobile number for urgent questions, although in practice the Friday screw delivery was the only emergency call of the programme.

## Delivery and logistics

A garage conversion in an occupied house needs to be unobtrusive. We structured the Princes Risborough deliveries around four priorities.

**Small, frequent drops.** Materials arrived on a 3.5-tonne tipper in quantities the team could carry straight into the garage, keeping the driveway clear. Each drop was sized to fit through the front door and around the corner into the garage without scratching the walls.

**Timed around work calls.** Because the owners worked from home, deliveries were booked for mid-morning to avoid their daily video calls. The driver also avoided honking the horn on arrival and carried materials through on dust sheets.

**Clean site.** Empty packaging and offcuts were collected on each return visit. The driveway was swept before the owners finished work. The team also wiped down the hallway floor after each delivery to remove any dust or mud. Empty nail and screw boxes were flattened and removed with the delivery vehicle so they did not pile up in the garage.

**Staged arrivals.** Battens and frame timber arrived first, then insulation, then plasterboard once the frame was complete. This kept the small garage from becoming overcrowded and meant each trade had space to work. The fixings were delivered in two batches so the team never had more boxes than they could store.

## The build

The build ran for 6 weeks.

- **Week 1: Strip and floor prep.** The garage was cleared, the existing door was removed and the concrete floor was ground level. A new threshold was formed where the door had been. The old door was taken away for recycling and the opening was boarded up temporarily.
- **Week 2: Frame and insulation.** A timber frame was built inside the single-skin walls, insulation was fitted and a vapour control layer was installed. The frame was built in sections so it could be carried through the house and assembled in the garage. Temporary bracing kept the frame square while the insulation and boards were fitted.
- **Week 3: Boarding.** Plasterboard was fixed to the walls and ceiling, and the opening where the door had been was lined and sealed. Moisture-resistant board was used on the wall that faced the garden.
- **Week 4: Electrics and heating.** Wiring, sockets, lighting, a wall heater and a through-wall vent were installed. The electrician added extra sockets for the rowing machine, TV and fan. A dedicated circuit was run from the main consumer unit to handle the combined load of the heater and fitness equipment.
- **Week 5: Finishes.** The walls were painted, the rubber floor was laid and a mirror was fitted on one wall. The rubber floor was laid in interlocking tiles so it could be replaced if any section wore out.
- **Week 6: Equipment and handover.** The rowing machine, weights rack and TV were installed. The gym was ready for the first workout. Widmer End walked through the room with the owners and demonstrated how to use the heater controls and the ventilation fan. They also left a small manual explaining how to adjust the fan speed for different types of workout.

The build finished on time. The owners started using the gym the following weekend and reported that the room stayed warm even on cooler September mornings. The through-wall vent kept the air fresh during workouts, and the rubber floor deadened the sound of weights being set down.

## The result

The finished gym is a warm, well-ventilated room with insulated walls, a rubber floor, a mirrored wall and space for a full workout. It gives the owners a dedicated fitness space without sacrificing any of the main house. The room faces north-east and stays cool in summer thanks to the insulation and the through-wall fan. In winter, the wall heater brings it up to temperature in under ten minutes. The couple now use the gym three or four times a week, which is more often than they expected.
