## The brief

Penn Build Co. was asked to convert a single-skin detached garage at the end of a garden in Newbury, within the RG14 postcode district, into a home gym. The clients were keen runners and cyclists who wanted a warm, ventilated space for weights, a turbo trainer and a small recovery area, with storage for bikes and kit. The husband was training for a marathon and the wife wanted a space for yoga and stretching, so the room had to be flexible enough for both purposes. They also wanted the gym to look like a finished room rather than a converted garage, with painted walls, proper lighting and a comfortable temperature all year round. This meant hiding the original garage structure behind a fully finished internal lining with no visible blockwork or concrete.

The garage was 6m by 3m with a cold concrete floor, no insulation and a metal up-and-over door that let in draughts. Penn Build planned to insulate the floor, walls and roof, board the interior, add electrics and ventilation, and fit a new personnel door and window. The clients wanted the gym ready before the summer holidays so they could start training indoors without being disturbed by the rest of the household. They also wanted to keep one wall free for a large mirror and storage hooks for bikes. The mirror wall needed a flat, even surface, which meant the plasterboard had to be finished to a high standard. The carpenter took extra care when fixing the boards to avoid any unevenness that would show through the mirror.

We supplied the fixings pack for the framing, boarding and joinery. The insulation, plasterboard and timber were sourced through Penn Build's main merchant, but the builder wanted the fixings on a separate Star Hawk account so he could track the job cost cleanly. Although the order was small, the builder wanted it on site before the carpenter started because a garage conversion has no space for a mid-morning merchant run. The clients also liked the idea that one supplier was handling the fixings, which reduced the number of deliveries to their home.

## The challenge

Garage conversions look simple but the details matter. Three things shaped this job.

1. **Single-skin walls.** The existing garage was built as a shed, not a room. The internal timber frame had to be built clear of the damp wall, with a vapour control layer and insulation behind the board. That meant a lot of fixing into concrete and timber in a small space. The galvanized nails were used for the timber battens and grounds, while the wood screws fixed the plasterboard and joinery. The carpenter also had to make sure the frame was plumb despite the original wall being out of true. This took longer than expected but was essential for a clean finish on the plasterboard.

2. **Garden access.** The only route to the garage was down the side of the house and through a gate. Deliveries had to be small enough to carry by hand or barrow, and timed so the family's cars were off the drive. The fixings were delivered in a small van that could park on the road without blocking the pavement. The driver carried the boxes down the side passage and stacked them neatly just inside the garage door. He also took away the empty carton from a previous delivery on the return leg.

3. **Ventilation.** A gym generates moisture and CO₂, so the design included a mechanical extract and trickle vents. The carpenter needed fixings for the vent ducts, the door frame and the equipment wall as well as the main boarding. The high-performance wood screws were supplied in a range of lengths so the carpenter had the right fixing for each application. The shorter screws were used for plasterboard and the longer screws for fixing timber to timber and the equipment battens to the block wall. The carpenter also used the screws to fix the window and door frames securely into the openings. The extract fan was sized to change the air in the room several times per hour during workouts. The trickle vents were positioned high on the wall so they would not create a draught across the training area.

The programme was six weeks because the clients wanted to use the gym before the summer holidays. The builder also had another job starting in Newbury in July, so any overrun on the gym would have affected the next project. This made the six-week deadline firm and left no room for material delays.

## The build

The build ran for 6 weeks on a compact, garage-focused programme.

- **Week 1: Strip and prepare.** The garage was cleared, the floor was levelled and a damp-proof membrane was laid. A new concrete screed went over insulation. The old metal door was removed and the opening was prepared for the new window and personnel door. The skip was filled with the old door, the cleared-out clutter and the excavated material from the floor preparation.

- **Week 2: Frame and insulation.** A timber frame was built inside the existing walls, leaving a ventilation gap. Insulation was fitted between studs and rafters. The carpenter used galvanized nails for the battens and wood screws for the frame joints. The frame was checked for plumb before the boards went on. Any gaps between the frame and the original wall were packed to prevent cold bridging.

- **Week 3: Boarding and vapour control.** Plasterboard was fixed to the frame, vapour control tape was applied at overlaps, and the new window and door openings were formed. The high-performance wood screws were used for the plasterboard because they gave a secure fixing without snapping. The galvanized nails were used for the timber grounds around the window and door openings.

- **Week 4: Electrics and ventilation.** First-fix electrics, LED lighting, heating and extract ventilation were installed. The carpenter fixed the vent ducting and the equipment battens using the longer screws from the reserve stock.

- **Week 5: Finishes.** The interior was decorated, rubber flooring was laid and the equipment-fixing battens were fixed to one wall. The builder tested the extract fan to make sure it could handle the moisture from a hard training session. The mirror wall was fitted on the final day after the floor was protected. The clients were impressed that the room looked like a professional gym rather than a converted outbuilding. They have since added a small fridge for drinks and a Bluetooth speaker.

- **Week 6: Equipment and handover.** The clients' weights, turbo trainer and bike racks were installed. The room was snagged and handed over. The clients did their first workout that evening and posted a photograph of the finished gym online. The builder received two enquiries from local homeowners who saw the post. One of those enquiries has since turned into a confirmed garage conversion project in the same area.

The gym was ready for use a week before the summer holidays. The only small change was the addition of an extra power socket for the turbo trainer, which the electrician fitted during week 4.

## The result

The finished gym is a warm, dry, well-ventilated room that the clients use almost every day. The rubber floor absorbs dropped weights, the equipment wall keeps bikes off the floor, and the extract fan prevents condensation during hard sessions. The clients said the best improvement was being able to train at home without the time and cost of driving to a gym in central Newbury.

Penn Build billed the clients £12,500 for the conversion. The Star Hawk fixings order was £530. The real benefit was reliability: the carpenter never had to stop for fixings, and the programme stayed on track.
