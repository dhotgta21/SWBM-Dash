## The brief

Loddon Build Co. was asked to carry out a full house refurbishment for a family in Reading who had bought a 1930s semi-detached house and wanted to update it from top to bottom before moving in. The brief covered new electrics, new plumbing, a new kitchen, two new bathrooms, re-plastering throughout, new doors and skirtings and a complete redecoration. The family also wanted to retain the original fireplaces and the parquet flooring in the hallway, which added a conservation element to the job. The fireplaces were carefully stripped and repointed, and the parquet was lifted, cleaned and relaid where it had become loose.

The family was renting nearby and wanted to move in before the start of the new school term, which gave Loddon a 12-week window. Because the house was empty, the builder could work room by room, but the programme still had to be tight because each trade followed the next in a fixed sequence. Any delay in boarding would push the plasterer, and any delay in plastering would push the decorator, so the material supply had to keep pace.

Loddon needed plasterboard, drylining battens, insulation and fixings delivered in small, frequent drops that matched the room-by-room schedule. They called us with the specification and we priced the boarding and fixings pack. The family knew the material cost before the first wall was taken back to brick. Loddon had used Star Hawk on previous Reading jobs and wanted the same reliable service for this larger project.

## The challenge

Full house refurbishments always create a particular set of pressures. On this Reading job we had to manage access, sequencing, dust control and the fixed move-in date. The school term start date was non-negotiable, so every week of the programme mattered.

**Access.** The house was on a narrow residential street in the RG1 area with permit parking and no off-street space. A full-size wagon could not stop outside. We used a 3.5-tonne tipper that could pull up briefly and carry materials through the front door on dust sheets. Deliveries were booked for mid-morning after the school run. The driver had a permit displayed and never blocked the pavement for more than a few minutes. Neighbours on the narrow street were able to come and go without disruption during deliveries.

**Sequencing.** A full house refurbishment is a multi-stage job. The schedule ran from stripping and first fix, through plastering and second fix, to decoration and final snagging. We split our deliveries into matched drops so the site never had surplus boards blocking the hallway, and each trade had exactly what they needed for the rooms they were working on. The schedule was updated every Friday for the following week. Loddon's site manager would call our scheduler with the planned rooms for the next week, and we would arrange the matching deliveries for Monday and Wednesday mornings.

**Plasterboard size.** The house had a small stairwell with a turn halfway up. Full 2.4m boards could not be carried up without flexing and damaging the corners. We supplied boards cut to 1.8m lengths for the upper floor and carried them up by hand. The shorter boards also reduced waste because fewer offcuts were needed to fill the small spaces in the bedrooms. Loddon estimated that the pre-cut boards reduced plasterboard waste by around 15% compared with standard lengths.

**Dust control.** Because the refurbishment involved taking every wall back to brick, dust travelled easily. We made sure deliveries were clean and that packaging was removed immediately so it did not add to the dust problem. The driver carried boards through on dust sheets and wiped the hallway after each delivery. This attention to detail meant the dust from the refurbishment was not spread around the house by the delivery process.

**Same-day contact.** Loddon's site manager had one named scheduler at Star Hawk. When the plasterer finished the upstairs a day ahead of schedule, we moved the downstairs plasterboard delivery forward so the team could keep going. This flexibility helped Loddon make up time and meant the family could move in as planned. It also demonstrated the value of having a supplier who could react to programme changes without charging for every small adjustment.

## Delivery and logistics

Refurbishments on narrow streets need a light touch. We structured the Reading deliveries around three priorities.

**Small vehicle.** Everything arrived on a 3.5-tonne tipper that could park legally for the short time needed to unload. The vehicle was small enough to reverse into a single parking bay if one was free.

**Timed drops.** Deliveries were booked to arrive just before the relevant trade started. Boards for the upstairs arrived the afternoon before the plasterer moved up. Boards for the kitchen arrived the day the old kitchen was removed. This phasing meant the plasterer never had to wait for materials, and the house never had surplus boards blocking the stairs. It also protected the boards from damage, because plasterboard left in a corridor can easily be knocked or creased.

**Clean site.** The driver carried materials through on dust sheets and removed empty pallets and wrapping with the vehicle. Loddon commented that the house stayed tidier than on previous jobs because there was never a build-up of packaging. The empty house also meant there was space to stack a small amount of material, but we still delivered little and often to avoid clutter. Loddon preferred this approach because it reduced the risk of boards being damaged before they were fixed.

## The build

The build ran for 12 weeks.

- **Week 1: Strip out.** The old kitchen, bathrooms, carpets, doors and skirtings were removed. Walls were taken back to brick where necessary and the ceilings were stripped. A skip was positioned outside and waste was removed as the strip progressed. The old plaster and lath generated a surprising amount of waste, and a second skip was brought in during week two.
- **Weeks 2–3: First fix.** New wiring, plumbing and heating pipework were run. Timber battens were added to walls that needed straightening. The battens were fixed with the galvanized nails supplied by Star Hawk.
- **Weeks 4–6: Boarding and plastering.** Plasterboard was fixed throughout the house and skimmed. The two bathrooms were tanked ready for tiling. Moisture-resistant boards were used in the bathrooms and standard boards elsewhere. The insulation was fitted behind the plasterboard on the external walls to improve the thermal performance of the 1930s house. This upgrade should reduce the family's heating bills and make the rooms more comfortable in winter.
- **Weeks 7–8: Second fix.** Sockets, switches, lights, radiators and door linings were fitted. The new kitchen and bathrooms started to take shape. The wood screws supplied by Star Hawk were used to fix the door linings and architraves. They were also used for the kitchen unit fixings and the bathroom vanity units.
- **Weeks 9–10: Tiling and flooring.** Bathrooms were tiled, floors were laid and doors were hung. The parquet floor in the hallway was sanded and sealed, preserving the original feature.
- **Weeks 11–12: Decoration and snagging.** The house was painted, snagged and cleaned. The family moved in the weekend before the school term started. Loddon handed over the keys on the Friday and the family spent the weekend unpacking boxes and arranging furniture. The children started at their new school on the Monday, and the parents were relieved that the move had happened on schedule.

The programme finished on time. The family collected the keys on the Friday before the school term started and spent the weekend unpacking. Loddon completed a final snagging list during the first week after the family moved in and returned to touch up a few paint marks.

## The result

The finished house is a fresh, modern family home with new electrics, plumbing, heating, kitchen, bathrooms and a clean, neutral decoration. The original fireplaces were retained and restored, giving the house character as well as modern comfort. The family were particularly pleased with the kitchen-diner, which had previously been a dark, narrow room but was now bright and open. The restored fireplaces in the reception rooms also received positive comments from visitors. The children were excited to have their own rooms freshly decorated before the new school term.

Loddon billed the family £78,000 for the refurbishment. The Star Hawk boarding and fixings pack was £490. The family felt the investment was worthwhile because the house was transformed from a tired 1930s property into a modern family home. They also noted that the house felt warmer and more comfortable thanks to the new heating system and the insulation behind the plasterboard.

For a material spend of £490, the value was in the precision. The right boards, cut to the right length, arrived on the right day so the plasterer never had to wait or make a merchant run. Loddon's site manager said that the single-supplier approach made the programme easier to manage than on previous jobs where materials had come from several merchants. He also appreciated that the delivery notes were emailed to the office within an hour of offload, making cost tracking straightforward.
