## The brief

Northgate Construction was called to a 1960s semi-detached house in Aylesbury where the original tiled roof had started to leak over the main bedroom and the upstairs hallway. The homeowners had lived with damp patches for two winters and had decided to act after a storm lifted several tiles and water began to run down the inside of the chimney breast. They wanted the roof stripped back to the rafters and re-covered with natural slate to match a neighbour's recent re-roof, and they needed the work finished before the February half-term because the family was going away. The February weather made the deadline tighter: any rain getting in during the strip would have damaged the ceilings and decoration they had already repaired once.

We priced the roofing schedule from the roofer's measurements, covering the breathable underlay, battens, fixings and dry ridge components that sit beneath and around the slate covering. The natural slate itself was sourced directly by Northgate's roofing supplier because the clients wanted a specific Welsh slate that was not part of our standard stock range. Our role was to make sure that everything the slaters needed apart from the slate tiles was on the scaffold at the right moment and in the right quantity. That included making small adjustments to the order if the roofer found extra rotten battens or needed a different gauge once the old covering was off.

The roof area was roughly 95 square metres, with two valleys, a chimney stack and a party-wall abutment that needed careful detailing. Northgate wanted a supplier who understood that a re-roof is a race against the weather and who could react if the scaffold schedule or slate delivery shifted. They had used merchants before who delivered to the kerb and left the roofers to carry materials up by hand, wasting valuable hours on a weather-sensitive job.

## The challenge

Re-roofs are among the most time-sensitive jobs in residential construction. Once the old covering is stripped away, the house is exposed, and any delay in the follow-on materials can leave the property vulnerable to rain. Four issues shaped this Aylesbury job, and each one influenced the delivery plan.

1. **Scaffold timing.** The scaffold was booked for a Monday morning and the old roof had to come off the same day. We coordinated our first drop of felt, battens and fixings to arrive at 7am, before the scaffold was fully signed off, so the roofers could start stripping immediately once the access was safe. The driver waited while the scaffold tag was checked so the materials could be lifted straight onto the boards.

2. **Matching the slate gauge.** The new slate was a different size and thickness to the old concrete tiles, so the batten spacing had to be recalculated on site. We supplied extra battens and additional nails so the roofers could adjust the gauge without a second merchant run. Having the spare material on the scaffold meant the slaters could keep working even when the gauge changed halfway up the roof.

3. **School-run access.** The road outside the house is busy between 8am and 9am and again at 3pm when parents collect children from the nearby primary school. All our drops were scheduled between 9.30am and 2.30pm to avoid blocking the street. The driver also avoided reversing across the pavement during drop-off and collection times.

4. **Chimney and valley detailing.** The two valleys carried a lot of water from the large front and rear roof slopes, and the chimney stack needed new lead flashings. The torch-on felt we supplied was used to line the valleys and dress the upstands, while the lead work was carried out by a specialist lead contractor. The detailing here was critical because the previous leaks had started at the chimney abutment and at the base of one valley, so the new work had to be faultless.

The homeowners were living in the house throughout, so noise, dust and security were all concerns. Northgate asked us to keep deliveries compact and to remove any packaging so the front garden did not become a storage yard. We also made sure that the driver did not block the driveway, so the family could still come and go during the working day. This small courtesy made a big difference to the homeowners' patience during a disruptive project.

## Delivery and logistics

Roofing materials have to be where the roofers are when they are ready, not where the driver finds it convenient to tip. We structured the Aylesbury drops around three practical rules that kept the job moving and the site safe.

- **Lift to scaffold.** The driver offloaded at the kerb and the roofers' labourer used the scaffold hoist to lift battens and rolls of felt up to eaves level. We kept the vehicle on site only as long as necessary, and the driver swept the pavement before leaving. Where possible the driver placed materials close to the hoist to reduce carrying distance.
- **Reserve pack.** We held a reserve of nails, screws and an extra roll of felt at the Aylesbury yard. Once a roof is stripped, you cannot afford to stop because a box of fixings has run out or a roll of felt is damaged during lifting. The reserve was called on once when a roll of felt was punctured by a nail left in the old battens.
- **Batch tracking.** The felt batch numbers and any treatment certificates for the battens were logged on the delivery note. Northgate's site manager needed this documentation for the client handover pack and for any future insurance or warranty claim.

All drops on the Aylesbury re-roof were confirmed the evening before and timed around the school run. When the Welsh slate delivery was delayed by a day because of snow on the quarry route, we immediately moved our reserve felt and fixing drop so the roofers could keep working on the valleys while they waited for the slates. That flexibility meant no paid downtime for the slating team and kept the four-week programme intact.

## The build

The build ran for four weeks, with each week focused on a distinct phase of the re-roof.

- **Week 1: Scaffold and strip.** The scaffold went up on the Monday and the old tiles and battens were stripped the same day. The rafters were inspected, any rotten sarking boards were replaced, and the roof was covered with a temporary tarp each evening. The DPM roll was used to protect exposed rafters where the old felt had disintegrated. Northgate found more rot than expected in two rafter feet, which delayed the battens by half a day but did not affect the overall programme.
- **Week 2: Underlay and battens.** On the Aylesbury roof, breathable membrane was draped over the rafters and counter-battens were fixed. New treated battens were spaced to the slate gauge and nailed down with galvanized nails. The valleys were lined with torch-on felt and the upstands were dressed ready for the lead contractor. The extra battens we had supplied proved useful when the roofer decided to tighten the gauge on the steeper rear slope.
- **Week 3: Slating.** The Welsh slate was laid from eaves to ridge, with careful cuts around the two valleys and the chimney. The roof began to take on its finished appearance. Waste was cleared from the scaffold daily so the gutters did not block if it rained. The slaters worked from one side to the other so the house was never fully exposed.
- **Week 4: Ridge, flashings and cleanup.** The dry ridge kit was fitted with wood screws, ridge tiles were bedded and the lead flashings were dressed around the chimney. The scaffold was struck, the site was swept and the roof was handed over watertight. Northgate walked the roof with the homeowner and explained the dry ridge system and the expected lifespan of the slate.

The roof was watertight before the family left for half-term, which was the critical success criterion. Northgate reported that the job would have taken an extra two days if they had had to send someone to a merchant for additional battens or fixings. The homeowners returned from their break to a dry house and a clean front garden.

The loft space below is now dry, the bedroom ceiling stains have not returned, and the homeowners have a roof that should last more than three decades. They also reported that the house feels warmer thanks to the new breathable membrane and the improved detailing at the eaves and around the chimney stack.
