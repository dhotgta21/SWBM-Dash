## Plan the layout and work out the cuts

Take the wall down on paper: the position of the bath, shower, basin, sockets, windows and any obstacle that affects tile placement. **Always work from a centred layout** — measure the wall width, divide by the tile width + joint, and adjust the starting line so you don't end up with a 20mm sliver of tile at one end.

Mark a vertical plumb line on the wall where the first column of whole tiles will land, and a horizontal **datum line** (set the height so you get whole tiles above and below a bath or basin where possible). Tiling normally starts about a course above the bath tub, so the bottom row sits just above the bath edge, behind the bath panel.

> **Common mistake:** starting tiling from the floor up. Bath tubs aren't level, baths can move, and any difference ends up as a wedge-shaped cut tile. Set the datum level and start there.

## Prep the wall

Tiles need a flat, clean, sound surface. **Plasterboard** can be tiled directly with a suitable adhesive. **Bare plaster** should be fully dry, primed with a diluted PVA or acrylic primer, and allowed to dry before tiling. **Painted walls** need to be sanded back to a key, with any flaking paint removed and the surface primed. **Old tiles** can be tiled over provided they're firmly fixed — wash them down with sugar soap, key them lightly with a belt sander, then prime before tiling.

Where the wall will get wet — inside the shower, around the bath — fit **tank** or **tile backer board** behind the tiles. Standard plasterboard doesn't like prolonged water contact and will swell and fail behind tiles within a few years.

## Mix the adhesive

For most ceramic wall tiles in dry areas, a **ready-mixed acrylic adhesive** is fine — it comes in a tub and is applied straight from the container.

For **porcelain tiles** (which absorb almost no water), **large-format tiles**, or anywhere that'll see water, use a powdered **C2-grade cement-based adhesive**. Mix it in a clean bucket with cold water using a paddle mixer on a slow drill — about 4 parts powder to 1 part water. Mix to the consistency of thick peanut butter, let it stand for 2 minutes, then mix again.

> **Tip:** only mix as much adhesive as you can use in **about 30 minutes** — it skins over and loses grab after that, especially in warm rooms.

## Fix the tiles

Starting at your datum line, spread adhesive on the wall with a **notched trowel** (10mm notches for standard tiles, 12mm for large-format). Hold the trowel at 45° and comb the adhesive into straight, even ribs — don't swirl. That gives you a uniform bed thickness and consistent grab.

Press the first tile firmly into place with a slight twist, lining up with both the vertical plumb line and the horizontal datum. **Place the second tile using spacers** between the tiles and your eye on the joint — 2mm spacers for tight joints, 3mm for the standard ceramic look, 5mm for hand-made or rustic tiles.

> **Tip:** run a spirit level across the top of each course. The wall may not be flat — a 1mm deviation now becomes a 6mm deviation in six courses if you don't check.

Work outwards from the datum in both directions, completing one course at a time. **Wipe any adhesive off the tile face** with a damp sponge before it sets — dried-on adhesive is the difference between a clean job and a day spent chipping residue off.

## Cut tiles around fittings

Mark each cut on the tile face using a felt-tip pen or chinagraph pencil. **Straight cuts:** score and snap with a manual tile cutter. **Thin slivers:** use a pair of tile nibblers to nibble away the waste in small bites — they crack if you try to take too much. **Curves around pipes:** use a diamond-tipped hole saw the same diameter as the pipe, drilled half from the front and half from the back to avoid breakout. **L-shapes around sockets:** mark carefully and use a wet saw, or make a series of parallel nibble cuts across the waste and snap.

Test-fit every cut before spreading adhesive, since a wrong cut can ruin a tile.

## Grout

Leave the adhesive for **at least 24 hours** before grouting. Mix the grout to the consistency of smooth peanut butter, following the maker's water ratios. **Unsanded grout** for joints under 3mm, **sanded grout** for joints over 3mm.

Spread grout diagonally across the tiles with a rubber squeegee, forcing it into every joint. Let it haze on the face of the tiles for 10–15 minutes, then wipe the excess off with a damp (not wet) sponge in diagonal strokes. Repeat with a clean sponge until the joints are smooth and the tile faces are clean.

Once the grout has firmed (about 30 minutes), polish the tile faces with a dry microfibre cloth to bring up the finish.

After the grout has cured for 24 hours, seal the **corners and around the bath or shower tray with silicone sealant**. Mask both edges with tape, run a 5–6mm bead, smooth it with a wet finger, peel the tape immediately. Don't leave silicone for the last minute of a Saturday afternoon — it can be messy the first time.

## Materials and tools checklist

**Materials**

- Wall tiles — 10% extra for cuts and breakages
- Tile adhesive (ready-mixed or powdered C2)
- Grout (unsanded for <3mm, sanded for >3mm)
- Tile spacers
- Silicone sealant (anti-mould for bathrooms)
- Acrylic primer

**Tools**

- Notched trowel
- Tile cutter (manual or electric wet saw)
- Tile nibblers
- Diamond hole saw (for pipework)
- Rubber grout float
- Sponges and a bucket
- Microfibre cloth
- Spirit level
- Tape (for silicone mask)

## When to call a tiler

A single bathroom wall is a confident-DIY project for a first-time tiler; an entire bathroom floor-to-ceiling in large-format porcelain is more involved. Get a tiler in if:

- The walls are very uneven and need a lot of dubbing out
- You're tiling a full wet-room (anywhere that gets wet)
- The tiles are large-format (over 600mm on a side)
- The substrate is timber-frame and needs careful detailing to prevent movement
- You're after a complex pattern (herringbone, mosaic, book-matched)
