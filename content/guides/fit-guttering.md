## Plan the run and the falls

Measure the total length of the roofline you're guttering. Plan the **high point** at the far end of the run and the **low point** at the end where the downpipe will go. The standard fall is **10mm per 6m of run** (1:600) towards the downpipe. For very long runs over 12m, fit two downpipes — one at each end — so the fall stays within sensible limits.

Pick the **size** of gutter (most domestic UK homes use 112mm half-round uPVC) and the **downpipe size** (typically 68mm round). Make a quick sketch with the positions of every bracket, union, stop-end, running outlet, and the downpipe run.

> **Tip:** when ordering, allow **one or two extra brackets** for fixing near the downpipe and at any bends. Also buy **lubricant spray** for the rubber seals — it makes assembling the joints a 30-second job rather than a fight.

## Fix the brackets

Brackets are screwed to the fascia board (the vertical board that runs along the roofline). Start by **snapping a chalk line** from the high point to the low point of the run, with the slope set at 10mm per 6m.

1. Fix the **first bracket** at the high point end. Set it so the top of the bracket lines up with the chalk line, not the bottom — guttering sits in the bracket, so the bracket position determines where the water edge falls.
2. Fix the **last bracket** at the low point end, lining up with the chalk line.
3. **Run a string line** between the two brackets to confirm the fall is correct along the whole length.
4. Fix **intermediate brackets** at every 600mm along the chalk line, with an extra bracket within 150mm of any joint or fitting.

> **Common mistake:** fixing the brackets into the rafter feet instead of the fascia. The fascia is the structural board — rafter feet are often overshot by the tiles and the bracket pulls out within a season. Drive the screws into solid timber, not air.

## Cut and fit the gutter lengths

Measure each run between brackets, then cut the **gutter length** to size using a fine-tooth saw or a specialist PVC gutter cutter. Cut square — a wonky cut shows at every joint. **Deburr the cut** with a knife or sandpaper so the rubber seal sits cleanly.

Lay a small bead of **silicone sealant** on each union before you push the lengths together, even though most modern brackets have rubber seals. As a general rule, double-seal every joint — they'll be the first place the gutter leaks in five years time.

Fit the gutter into the brackets, **clip each length into place** through the bracket, and check that the downpipe-running-outlet aligns with where the downpipe will fall on the wall below.

## Fit the downpipe

The downpipe runs from the **running outlet** (the fitting that slots into the gutter) down the wall to the drain or soakaway. Mark its position on the wall, then fix **downpipe brackets** every 1.5m or so along the run.

Cut the downpipe lengths with a fine-tooth saw and assemble them with **swaged ends upward** so the upper length sockets into the lower one (water flows through the joint, not into it). At the bottom, fit a **45° shoe** to direct the flow into the drain gully or hopper.

If the downpipe goes underground into the drain, **make sure the connection rod-able** — most UK installations use a standard 110mm PVCu drain pipe with a slip coupling. Put a leaf-guarding balloon in the drain while you work; the only thing worse than a blocked downpipe is a blocked drain at the same time.

## Test the run

Wait for the next steady rain, or **pour a bucket of water** slowly into the gutter at the high end. Walk the run and watch every joint. Any drip is worth fixing now — once a gutter leaks it's hard to find the exact joint without taking the run apart.

> **Tip:** drips at the corners usually mean the gutter is sitting too high at the low-point end or the bracket is out of plumb. Slacken the bracket screws, adjust the height, retighten, and the leak usually disappears.

## Materials and tools checklist

**Materials**

- uPVC half-round gutter lengths (typically 4m)
- Gutter union brackets (one per joint)
- Gutter brackets (one per 600mm)
- Running outlet
- Stop-ends
- Downpipe lengths
- Downpipe brackets (every 1.5m)
- 45° downpipe shoe (or connection to drain)
- Silicone sealant
- Stainless-steel or coated screws
- Lubricant spray

**Tools**

- Tape measure, pencil, chalk line
- Spirit level
- Fine-tooth saw or PVC gutter cutter
- Drill and screwdriver bits
- Stanley knife (for deburring cuts)
- Ladder or scaffold tower

## When to call a roofer

A single straight run of guttering on a single-storey extension is a fine DIY project; a multi-storey guttering replacement with several corners and awkward joins is more involved. Get a roofer in if:

- The fascia is rotten and needs replacing as well as the gutter
- The guttering is two or more storeys up (ladders and safety)
- The downpipe enters the underground drainage and needs a new connection
- The roofline is awkward (over conservatories, around bay windows, etc.)
